About

A trait-based view of plant–environment relationships

My research focuses on the relationship between environmental variation and plant functional traits—including morphological, physiological and biochemical traits—to understand community assembly, plant adaptation strategies and the effects of climate change on plant communities.

I study trait variation across spatial and organisational scales, scaling relationships among traits, and trait responses along environmental gradients. My work particularly focuses on high-altitude and high-latitude ecosystems because of their harsh and heterogeneous environments, vulnerability to climate change and distinctive biodiversity.

Research interests

  • Plant functional traits and community assembly
  • Climate-change impacts on plants and ecosystems
  • Alpine and subalpine ecology
  • Plant adaptation and stress responses
  • Taxonomic, functional and phylogenetic diversity
  • Species distributions and ecological niches
  • Conservation of rare and threatened plants

Research

Questions I work on

My research combines natural gradients, experiments and long-term ecological perspectives.

01

Climate change & plant functional ecology

How warming, drought and other environmental changes alter plant traits, community strategies and ecosystem functioning.

02

Alpine & mountain ecosystems

Understanding plant responses and adaptation in high-altitude environments characterized by strong environmental heterogeneity.

03

Biodiversity & community assembly

Examining the abiotic and biotic drivers of species distributions, diversity, community structure and extinction risk.

04

Trait variation & adaptation

Connecting trait variation across individuals, populations and communities to environmental gradients and plant strategies.

05

Species distribution & conservation

Using ecological niche modelling and climate scenarios to investigate future distributions of endemic, rare and threatened plants.

06

Emerging integrative approaches

Integrating ecological experiments with physiological, biochemical and statistical approaches to develop mechanistic understanding.
